How it works

Every morning at 11:00 ET the model pulls live odds from around 17 sportsbooks, the official injury report, rosters, play-by-play and the forecast at kickoff, rebuilds every team's rating, and projects a score for each game.

Edges. The edge is the model's line minus the book's. A spread needs 3.5 points to be flagged, a total needs 3. Four or more (4.5 on totals) makes it 🔥 STRONG and two units. A spread the model likes to cover 68% of the time — about 6.4 points of disagreement — or a total past 7, is a 💎 MAX, three units, which should turn up about once a week. That 68% is the model's own arithmetic, not a measured hit rate; the calibration page is where you find out if picks it calls 68% actually land 68% of the time. Bigger edges have never been shown to win more often, so treat the size as how much the model disagrees, not as how sure it is.

Units. Bets are sized to win, not to risk: STRONG is 2 units, SOLID is 1. At -110 a loss costs 1.1 times the target.

One game day at a time. Picks only post for the next day with games, so a Thursday board shows Thursday night only. Lines and injuries move too much for a Sunday pick made on Tuesday to mean much.

Grading. Finished games are graded automatically against the number that was posted, and the closing line is recorded, so the record can be judged on more than wins and losses.

What it isn't. It is not beating the market yet. Measured against closing lines, the model's average miss is about the same as the book's. Bet accordingly.
